Standard vs Premium Coconut Product Specifications: A Practical Guide for Importers

A clear specification is more useful than a vague grade label. Learn what to compare, what evidence to request and which details should be agreed before placing an order with an Indonesian supplier.

What does “standard” and “premium” mean in coconut product sourcing?

In international trade, the words standard and premium are often used as commercial descriptions. They do not automatically refer to one universal grade, certification or legally defined specification. A premium requirement usually means tighter limits, a narrower tolerance, more consistent appearance or additional testing agreed by the buyer and supplier.

Compare the same test method and reporting basis

Two laboratory values should not be compared unless the sampling procedure, analytical method, sample preparation and reporting basis are understood. This is especially important for ash and moisture in charcoal products, electrical conductivity (EC) in cocopeat, and free fatty acid (FFA) in coconut oils.

Ask for lot-specific evidence

Request a current certificate of analysis (COA) or test report that identifies the product, lot or batch, date, tested parameters, methods and responsible laboratory. A general brochure or an old sample report does not prove that a future shipment will have the same result.

Define acceptance before production or shipment

For critical parameters, agree the sampling plan, inspection party, test method, acceptance threshold, retest process and remedy for non-conformity in the contract or purchase documents. Avoid leaving quality disputes to interpretation after cargo arrival.

Product-specific considerations

  • Coconut shell charcoal briquettes: ash, moisture, volatile matter, fixed carbon, calorific value, dimensions and burning behaviour.
  • Coconut shell charcoal: size distribution, moisture, ash, volatile matter, fixed carbon and fines.
  • Cocopeat: pH, EC, moisture, expansion, compression, particle size and fibre content.
  • Coconut coir fibre: length, moisture, pith/dust, foreign matter and application-specific strength.
  • VCO: identity and process, FFA, moisture, peroxide value, aroma and destination compliance.
  • RBD coconut oil: FFA, moisture, peroxide value, colour, odour and melting profile.
  • Crude coconut oil: FFA basis, moisture and impurities, sediment, fatty-acid profile and intended use.

How to read COCOVANTA Standard and Premium targets

COCOVANTA has positioned the Standard column as a stronger commercial sourcing brief than a loose entry-level description. Where lower values generally indicate better control (for example moisture, ash, electrical conductivity, free fatty acid or peroxide value), the Standard target is set lower. Where higher values are generally desirable (for example fixed carbon), the target is set higher. For parameters where direction is application-dependent—such as pH, volatile matter, fibre length, colour, aroma, melting profile or expansion—the requirement is defined by a suitable range, buyer application or approved sample rather than assuming that a higher or lower number is always better.

For coconut shell charcoal briquettes, the COCOVANTA Standard ash target is now ≤ 2.2%; the Premium target remains ≤ 1.8%. This is a buyer-facing commercial target, not a claim that every supplier or current lot can meet it. COCOVANTA will request supplier confirmation and relevant test evidence before representing a grade as available.
